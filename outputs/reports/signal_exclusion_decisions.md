# Signal exclusion — the decision artifact

**Result: 40 → 49 exclusions. Model channels 62 → 53. Nothing is re-introduced.**

The three measurement reports deliberately carry no exclusion labels and no
recommendations — a measurement that already knows the answer cannot audit it.
This is the file that decides, and it cites them.

Per-signal before/after for all 102 signals: `decisions_reconciliation.md`
(generated by `QC/build_decisions_reconciliation.py`, not transcribed by hand).

Every figure quoted here is reproducible from a script in `QC/`. Superseded
analyses are kept and marked, never deleted — `all_can_eta2.md` and
`QC/verify_all_can_eta2.py` both carry a SUPERSEDED banner naming what replaced
them, because the reasoning history is part of the deliverable.

---

## The rule this follows

> **Statistics screen · physics decides · empirics verify.**

Three consequences, and the second is the one that does the work:

1. **A statistic alone never excludes a signal.** A high η² says "this separates
   drivers", not "this is a leak". Engine speed would separate drivers beautifully
   if they drove at different speeds — that is the behaviour we want.
2. **A statistic alone never *keeps* a signal either.** This is the asymmetry that
   the previous audit missed. A low score is not a clean bill of health; it means
   that particular instrument found nothing. Nine of the thermal exclusions score
   below 0.2 on both η² scales and are excluded anyway, on mechanism.
3. **Physics may exclude on its own.** A cumulative odometer is a trip label by
   construction; no measurement is needed to know that, and none could overturn it.

## What this decision rests on

| Input | What it supplies |
|---|---|
| `continuous_separability.md` | 81 signals — η² raw, η² per-window-mean, MI share |
| `step_separability.md` | 15 signals — Cramér's V, Theil's U |
| `event_presence.md` | 6 signals — firing rate, spread across drivers, position in drive |
| `signal_classification.md` | which of the three each signal is, over all 46 drivers |
| `signal_value_distributions.md` | value distribution and **per-driver constancy**, screened over all 102 signals |
| `DriverIDSQLiteCSVDataDictionary.pdf` | the authoritative SPN definitions and null-sentinel encodings |
| **C1 — one drive per driver** | the confound that makes "driver" and "day" the same variable |

**C1 is load-bearing throughout.** Each subject drove once, so any quantity that
tracks the *day* — ambient temperature, humidity, how long the truck had sat —
separates drivers perfectly while containing no behaviour at all. This is why
mechanism outranks measurement here: the statistic cannot tell a driving habit
from a weather report, and on this dataset it never will be able to.

## Part 1 — the 40 existing exclusions, re-examined

All 40 stand. Three groups were already on solid ground; one group's stated basis
was wrong and is corrected; one group has a soft spot that is named below.

### 1a. Counters, running averages, trip-state levels — 13 signals ✅ confirmed

245, 244, 918, 250, 182, 247, 235, 249, 236 (counters) · 185, 1029 (averages) ·
96, 1761 (levels). η² from 0.998 to 1.000; MI up to 1.000.

Physics is decisive without the statistic: an odometer reading *is* the identity of
the trip. These need no further defence.

### 1b. The six event signals — ⚠️ basis corrected

898, 518, 4191, 4206, 4207, 3544. Previously listed partly under "leaks" and partly
under "data quality". **The leakage framing was wrong, and `event_presence.md` now
shows why:** η² on firing rate is 0.106 for five of them and 0.061 for 3544. Their
firing patterns do not separate drivers at all.

They are excluded on **data quality and physics**, not leakage:

- absent 83–92% of the drive, with no interpolable support — filling them
  fabricates activity that never occurred;
- 4206 (message counter) and 4207 (message checksum) are **protocol metadata**, not
  vehicle state, so they could never be behaviour whatever they contained;
- all five of 4191/518/4206/4207/898 produce **byte-identical statistics across six
  independent measures over all 46 drivers**, which identifies them as fields of a
  single TSC1 message rather than five signals. The input set was triple-counting
  one message.

**Nothing of value is lost.** That is now measured rather than assumed.

⚑ *Open, unchanged:* four of these hold values their SPN definitions forbid
(4206 = 1111 and 4207 = 1111 in 4-bit fields; 4191 = 138 in a 3-bit field). The
likeliest cause is a decoder artifact in the CSV export. It does not affect this
decision — they are excluded either way — but if the decode is wrong for these
columns it may be wrong for others, which is a question about the whole dataset.
**Action: obtain the DBC.**

### 1c. Audit iteration 1 — 5 signals ✅ confirmed

3721, 5466, 3031, 171, 1172. η² 0.937–0.986. DEF tank temperature, ambient air
temperature, turbocharger intake temperature, and two DPF regeneration-history
quantities. Ambient state and accumulated service history; flagged loudly by the
statistic and confirmed by mechanism.

### 1d. The thermal / ambient / electrical family — 16 signals ⚠️ confirmed, with a named soft spot

105, 110, 177, 412, 1136, 3241, 3242, 3246, 4360, 4363, 4765, 5862, 108, 168, 975, 986.

Five are flagged by their own statistic: 168 Battery Potential (window η² **0.814**),
108 Barometric Pressure (**0.807**), 105 Intake Manifold Temperature (**0.755**),
1136 ECU Temperature (**0.569**), and 975 Engine Fan 1 Estimated Percent Speed
(**Cramér's V 0.565** — the highest value in the entire step report). Two are
mid-range: 177 Transmission Oil Temperature (0.378), 986 Fan Requested (V 0.316).

**🟡 The soft spot, stated plainly: nine of the sixteen score below 0.2 on both η²
scales** — 110, 412, 3241, 3242, 3246, 4360, 4363, 4765, 5862, the downstream
exhaust and SCR temperatures. Engine coolant temperature is the lowest in the
family at 0.078 raw / 0.102 window.

They are excluded on mechanism: every one is a temperature whose level is set by
ambient conditions plus how long the engine has been running, which under C1 is the
day, which is the driver. The supporting empirical claim is the **74% → 29%**
collapse when the family was removed — a 45-point drop that no individual η² in this
group predicts, which is the signature of many weak leaks combining.

**That empirical claim is now owed a re-verification, and it may not survive in its
current form.** The 74% → 29% number was attributed to this family, but
`continuous_separability.md` establishes that the leakiest thermal members
(DEF tank 0.986, ambient air 0.968, turbo intake 0.941) sit in group **1c**, which
was removed in the *previous* iteration. How much of the 45 points belonged to 1c
rather than 1d is **not currently known**. See Part 5.

A correction to the record that follows from this: `all_can_eta2.md` claimed the
family "scored 0.08–0.38" on raw η², implying the statistic was blind to it. The
family actually spans **0.078 to 0.986**. The 0.08–0.38 range describes only the
downstream exhaust temperatures. **The 74% → 29% collapse was not a failure of the
statistic — it was a failure to act on what the statistic already said.** That
report is now marked superseded and carries the correction.

## Part 2 — the nine new exclusions

### ➕ 5015 Momentary Engine Maximum Power Enable — constant

**One value across 12,931,907 samples and 46 of 46 drivers**, while transmitting on
99.6% of the drive. Cramér's V is undefined (one state); η² and MI are both 0/0.

Excluded as a **data-quality fix, not a leakage judgement** — with zero variance
there is no behaviour in it to lose, whatever the cause.

> **Worth recording, because the reasoning error is instructive.** This signal was
> proposed as a strong aggressive-driving feature, and *in principle that is exactly
> right*: a driver requesting maximum engine power is close to a direct measurement
> of aggression. On this truck the field never leaves one value. A feature's
> theoretical merit is not evidence that it exists in your data — which is the whole
> reason the measurement step comes before the selection step.

### ➕ 3229, 3230, 3231, 3232 — the aftertreatment sensor-status group

| Measurement | Value |
|---|---|
| "not available" rate per driver | **0.00% – 26.18%** (mean 9.99%) |
| mean position of unavailable samples in the drive | **0.104** (sd 0.088), 0 = start |
| share of unavailable samples in the first 10% of the drive | **57.7%** |
| all four fields unavailable together (Jaccard) | **1.000** for 27/46 drivers; the other 19 have none |
| Cramér's V | 0.189 – 0.306 |

These are sensor-readiness flags on the NOx and wide-range-oxygen sensors
downstream of the SCR. "Not available" means the sensor heater has not yet brought
the element to operating temperature. **How long that takes is set by ambient
temperature and how long the truck sat before the drive** — so the duration of the
unavailable period is a property of the day. Under C1 the day is the driver.

**An independent confirmation of the same 19 drives.** Screening all 102 signals for per-driver constancy shows these four — and 3239 — never change state in **19 of the 46** drives. The sentinel scan found all four fields sentinel-together in **27 of 46**, with the remaining 19 carrying no sentinel at all. Those are the same 19: the sentinel *is* the second state, so a drive without one is a drive where the signal never moved. Two measurements built from different quantities partition the cohort identically, which is what a real mechanism looks like.

The decisive comparison is with 2979, which is unavailable **more** often (28.57%)
and is harmless: its rate is 28.44–28.60% for every one of the 46 drivers, a
deterministic protocol cycle that cannot identify anyone. **A high missingness rate
is not the warning sign; a varying one is.** This group ranges from 0% to 26% and
clusters at the start of the drive — a warm-up duration, which is trip identity.

Is there any behaviour in it? Marginally — a driver who accelerates hard from cold
heats the exhaust faster. But that is inseparable from the ambient start
temperature, and with one drive per driver it cannot be separated even in principle.

3232 has an **independent and sufficient reason**: it is an FMI, an enumerated
J1939 *failure-mode identifier*. A diagnostic code is not driver behaviour by
definition, whatever its missingness pattern.

### ➕ 3239 Aftertreatment 2 Intake Dew Point — a weather variable

Step, 2 states, Cramér's V 0.305 🟡.

**Dew point is literally meteorology** — the temperature at which the ambient
humidity condenses. Ambient air temperature (SPN 171) is the other half of the same
ambient state and is already excluded at η² 0.968. There is no mechanism by which
driving behaviour sets the dew point at an aftertreatment inlet.

Excluded on physics. The 🟡 is consistent with that but is not the reason.

### ➕ 2978 Estimated Engine Parasitic Losses – Percent Torque — the thermal family in disguise

**η² raw 0.244 → window 0.665.** The highest-scoring kept continuous signal, and
the second-largest raw-to-window gap in the dataset (+0.421).

**The data dictionary names the mechanism outright**, so this is a citation rather
than an inference:

> "the estimated amount of torque loss due to engine parasitics, such as **cooling
> fan, air compressor, air conditioning**, etc. It is expressed as a percent of Engine
> Reference Torque."
> — `DriverIDSQLiteCSVDataDictionary.pdf`, SPN 2978

Every accessory it lists is thermal or electrical load. On a fixed vehicle these do not
vary with *how you drive* — they vary with how hard the thermal-management system is
working and what the electrical load is. **That is the same mechanism as SPN 975
Engine Fan 1 Estimated Percent Speed (V = 0.565) and SPN 168 Battery Potential
(window η² 0.814)**, both already excluded as members of this family.

So 2978 is a thermal-family member that survived the earlier audit for two reasons:
its raw η² (0.244) did not flag it, and its name does not contain the word
"temperature". Excluded on physics, surfaced by the window scale.

> **This is the one decision the second η² scale earned.** Of the 11 signals where
> window η² exceeds raw by ≥ 0.15, exactly one changes a verdict, and it is this one.
> (168 has a larger gap but was already excluded.) Worth stating honestly: the
> second scale was argued for as a major methodological addition and delivered a
> single signal. Mutual information delivered **none** — no signal has MI ≥ 0.5 while
> both η² columns sit below 0.5, and MI is systematically *lower* than η² on this
> data, so it behaved as a weaker detector rather than a complementary one. It is
> retained in the report as a recorded negative result, not as a criterion that
> earned its place.

### ➕ 4154 Actual Engine – Percent Torque (Fractional) — the decode does not honour its own definition

This signal was a **kept channel on the watch list** until its definition was checked
against the dataset's own dictionary. The dictionary is unambiguous:

> "When the MSB is set to 1, this parameter is not available. When the MSB is set to
> 0, bits 1 to 3 indicate the desired torque with resolution of **0.125%/bit**.
> **Range is from 0 to 0.875% of reference torque.** […] used in combination with
> SPN 513 Actual Engine Torque. The resulting actual engine torque will be calculated
> by adding these two parameters."
> — `DriverIDSQLiteCSVDataDictionary.pdf`, SPN 4154

So the legal engineering values are the eight multiples of 0.125% from 0 to 0.875%.
Measured over 46 drivers and 6,465,139 samples:

| Value | Share | Legal? |
|--:|--:|---|
| `0.0` | 90.6871% | ✅ |
| `13.0` | 2.1886% | ❌ **15×** the legal maximum |
| `125.0` | 6.5232% | ❌ **143×** the legal maximum |
| `138.0` | 0.6011% | ❌ **158×** the legal maximum |

**9.31% of samples lie outside the published range.** Only `0.0` is a legal value, so
the column does not represent the quantity it is named for.

Two further details make a decoding fault the only plausible reading:

1. The observed set is **exactly {0, 13, 125, 13+125}** — four values forming all sums
   of two components. That is the signature of bit-packing or a masking error, not of a
   physical measurement.
2. **`138.0` is also the single constant value held by SPN 4191** (Engine Requested
   Torque, Fractional), which the classification report already flags as impossible
   for its 3-bit field. Two columns of the same family carrying the same illegal value
   is a decoder fault, not two coincidences.

Excluded on **data quality**, on exactly the grounds already applied to 4191, 4206 and
4207. This also retires the type question for it: an excluded signal needs no
aggregation treatment.

> **The physics override on this signal was correct, and checking it is what found the
> defect.** `SIGNAL_REVIEW` recorded 4154 as continuous rather than step with the
> reason "percent torque (fractional adder), 0.125% resolution". The dictionary
> confirms that wording almost verbatim, and an external reference agrees the
> fractional values are multiples of 0.125 that sum with SPN 513. The override was
> right about the **SPN**; what no one had checked was whether the **column** honours
> it. It does not.

### ➕ 515 Engine's Desired Operating Speed — an ECU setpoint, 99.22% constant

Measured over all 46 drivers, 6,465,503 samples, 801 distinct values spanning
600–1300 (`QC/verify_signal_value_distributions.py`):

| Value | Share | Reading |
|--:|--:|---|
| **1300.0** | **99.2193%** | the governed setpoint — a dwell point |
| 600.0 | 0.0619% | idle — the only other dwell point |
| 791 values in 600–1300 | 0.6990% total, ~0.0024% each | transit between the two |

**Three reasons, any one of which would be sufficient:**

1. **It is not a driver input.** The driver commands torque through the pedal
   (SPN 91 → 512/513). *Desired operating speed* is what the ECU wants, set by the
   idle governor, PTO and cruise logic. A driver cannot address this parameter.
2. **There is almost nothing in it.** 99.22% of 6.47M samples hold a single value.
3. **The variation that exists is a recording-boundary artifact, not behaviour.**
   The distribution has exactly **two dwell points** (1300 and 600) with 791
   near-equally-populated values between them and no third preferred value. That is
   the signature of a **ramp** between idle and the setpoint, not of setpoint
   selection — a cruise-control engagement would dwell at whatever speed was chosen,
   producing additional peaks, and there are none. Whether such a ramp was captured
   depends on when logging started and stopped relative to engine start and
   shutdown. **9 of the 46 drives never leave 1300 at all**, so "was the ramp
   recorded" is a per-driver binary splitting 9 against 37 — which is why raw η² is
   0.023 while window η² is **0.366**: the ramp is rare enough to be diluted across
   raw samples, but the windows containing it are distinctive.

Point 3 is an **inference** from the shape of the distribution, labelled as one.
Points 1 and 2 are not, and they decide it on their own.

## Part 3 — kept subject to a precondition

### ⚠️ 2979 Vehicle Acceleration Rate Limit Status — keep, but unusable until fixed

**On leakage it is clean.** Cramér's V 0.117 🟢. Its unavailable samples run at
28.44–28.60% for all 46 drivers, at mean position exactly 0.500 with sd 0.000 —
exactly 2/7 of samples, a deterministic protocol cycle. A constant-rate artifact is
the opposite of a fingerprint.

**On data quality it is currently broken.** `aggregation_validity.md` measures
**40.2% of its aggregated values as illegal, across 100% of windows, with 4,793
transitions per window.** The cause is in the pipeline, not the signal: linear
interpolation across the "not available" sentinel manufactures states that never
occurred on the bus, and every sentinel-to-valid boundary is counted as a shift.

**Precondition for keeping it, both parts required:**

1. sentinel → `NaN` before aggregation (not a value to average);
2. zero-order hold instead of linear interpolation, per its step type.

Until both are in place this signal must be **excluded on data quality**. The
decision is "keep", the status is conditional, and the condition is testable.

## Part 4 — watch list

Named here so they cannot be quietly forgotten, with the specific test that would
resolve each. **No status change now** — none has a mechanism argument strong enough
to cut on, and none has a statistic above threshold.

| Flag | SPN | Signal | Concern | The test that resolves it |
|:-:|--:|---|---|---|
| 🟡 | 3251 | DPF Differential Pressure | raw 0.127 → window 0.327; the baseline is **soot load = accumulated trip history**, same family as 3721/5466 (both excluded at ≥ 0.937), while the dynamic part is exhaust flow = behaviour | η² on the window mean after removing a per-drive linear trend; if the leak is the slow soot baseline, detrending kills it |
| 🟡 | 514 | Nominal Friction – Percent Torque | **the dictionary confirms the thermal coupling**: it "contains the frictional and *thermodynamic* loss of the engine itself, pumping torque loss (SPN 5398), and the losses of fuel, oil and *cooling* pumps" — the same loss-torque family as the excluded 2978. Kept because the statistic disagrees: η² 0.036/0.132, against 2978's 0.665. Friction torque is also genuinely load-dependent, so it has behavioural content | within-drive correlation against SPN 110 coolant temperature |
| 🟡 | 5398 | Estimated Pumping – Percent Torque | same concern as 514 (0.042/0.148), **and the two are not independent** — confirmed from both dictionary entries: 5398 says its value "is included in SPN 514", and 514 says it contains "pumping torque loss (SPN 5398)". One is a component of the other | same; if 514 is kept, 5398 is partly redundant with it |
| 🟡 | 523, 524 | Transmission Current / Selected Gear | V = 0.117 looks clean, **but Cramér's V reads a contingency table and cannot see ordering.** Two drivers who occupy the same gears for the same total time score identically whether one shifted twice or two hundred times. Gear averages ~7.6 transitions per 120 s window here, so *shifting behaviour* is invisible to this statistic | transition counts and time-in-state per window — this is a **feature-engineering** task, not a re-measurement |
| 🟢 | 3216, 3226 | Engine Exhaust / Aftertreatment NOx | combustion chemistry responds to load (behaviour) but also to SCR dosing state | η² after the typed aggregation lands |
| 🟡 | 2945, 4816 | Active Shift Console Indicator · Torque Converter Lockup Transition | **constant in 3 of 46 drives**, 98.3% one value, 1–2 distinct. Kept because both are transmission and driver-actuated — no ambient or recording mechanism can flatten them, so flatness is behaviour, unlike 515. But a **3-against-43 split on a 98.3%-constant channel is the shape of a feature that identifies three drivers perfectly and generalises to nothing** | feature importance in Experiment 1; high rank despite this constancy is the warning sign |
| 🟡 | 51 | Engine Throttle Valve 1 Position 1 | one value for **98.35%** of a typical drive, and the lowest-scoring signal in the continuous report (η² 0.001 / 0.040). Varies in all 46 drives, so no per-driver label — not a leak, just close to carrying nothing | drop if Experiment 1 shows zero importance; costs a channel either way |

**523/524 is the important row.** Their low V is not evidence that gear carries no
driver information — only that gear *occupancy* does not. The behavioural content of
a gear channel is in its transitions, and no statistic in any of the three reports
measures that. Transition rate is the categorical counterpart of the continuous
spread column: occupancy says where the signal sits, transitions say how it moves.
The dictionary helps here: 523 is "the gear currently engaged … **transitions toward
a destination gear will not be indicated**", while 524 is "the gear that the
transmission will attempt to achieve during the current shift". So 523 steps cleanly
from engaged gear to engaged gear, and 524 *leads* it — which makes the 524→523 lag a
shift-dynamics feature in its own right, not a redundancy.

It needs the time-ordered state stream, which a contingency table has already
discarded, so it is a separate instrument rather than another column — and it is a
**feature** question, not an exclusion one. A high transition rate on gear would be
the behaviour this project wants, not a leak. The one state signal whose churn
*would* have leaked is fan cycling, whose rate is set by ambient temperature, and
both fan signals are already excluded — 975 caught by occupancy at V = 0.565.

## Part 5 — what must happen before this reaches `src/config.py`

This list is **not** to be written into `EXCLUDE_SPNS` and left there. A drop list was
once accepted in this project that collapsed the usable channel set to 4 signals;
the guard against a repeat is that every change to what the model sees carries
before/after control numbers. See `.claude/skills/dimensionality-change/SKILL.md`.

### The one rule that matters most here

> **Do not change the channel set and the aggregation in the same step.**

Both changes are pending. Bundled, a drop in accuracy cannot be attributed to
either, and neither number is interpretable. They run as two separate experiments,
in this order — the config change first because it is a one-line edit that reuses
the existing windowing.

### Experiment 1 — the channel set (62 → 53)

Current interpolation unchanged. `RandomForestClassifier` **and**
`HistGradientBoostingClassifier`, identical splits, seeds pinned.

| Arm | Channels | Purpose |
|---|--:|---|
| A | 62 | the current published baseline, reproduced |
| B | 53 | this decision applied |

2979 stays excluded in both arms: its precondition is unmet under interpolation.

### Experiment 2 — the thermal attribution the record owes

This is the one the 74% → 29% claim depends on, and it decides whether Part 1d's
nine physics-only exclusions keep their empirical support. Group 1c is the five
high-η² thermal signals removed in the earlier iteration; group 1d is the sixteen
removed in the later one.

| Arm | Removed | What its number tells us |
|---|---|---|
| T0 | counters and event signals only | the leak-inflated ceiling |
| T1 | T0 + group **1c** (5 signals, η² 0.937–0.986) | how much the *flagged* thermal signals were worth |
| T2 | T1 + group **1d** (16 signals) | how much the *family argument* was worth, on its own |

T1 → T2 is the number currently missing. If that gap is small, then the 45 points
belonged to 1c, the family argument has no empirical support, and the nine
sub-0.2 exclusions rest on mechanism alone — which is permissible under the rule at
the top of this file, but must then be *stated* as such rather than credited to an
experiment that measured something else.

### Experiment 3 — typed aggregation (separate change, after 1 and 2)

`build_windows.py:102` applies `np.interp` unconditionally to all channels. That is
correct for continuous signals and wrong for the other two types:

| Type | Count | Correct treatment |
|---|--:|---|
| continuous | 81 | linear interpolation onto the grid — current behaviour, already right |
| step | 15 | **zero-order hold**, plus transition count, time-in-state, mode |
| event | 6 | **do not fill**; presence/rate only (all 6 are excluded, so moot for now) |
| accumulators | — | last − first within the window, never a mean |

This is where SPN 2979's precondition is satisfied, and where the gear channels'
transition features arrive — the Part 4 watch item that no current statistic can
resolve. Only after this does 2979 re-enter as a kept channel.

### Not to be done before the above

- `src/config.py` stays as it is until Experiment 1 has produced both arms.
- `processed_data` is not regenerated until the exclusion list is final.
- The decoder fault behind 4154, 4191, 4206 and 4207 is a **dataset-wide** question,
  not only a reason to drop four columns. If the decode is wrong for these it may be
  wrong for others. **Action: obtain the DBC**, and treat this as a stated limitation
  in the writeup either way.
